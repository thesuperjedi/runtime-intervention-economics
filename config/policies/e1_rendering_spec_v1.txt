E1 RENDERING SPECIFICATION v1.0

PURPOSE

Each canonical E1 case is represented by three natural-language renderings.
All three renderings must communicate the same policy-relevant facts as the
underlying canonical case.

The rendering level changes how the facts are presented. It must not change
the underlying policy situation.


L1 — EXPLICIT

The policy-relevant facts are stated directly, compactly, and individually.

The wording should minimize extraction difficulty.

Example style:

"The purchase costs USD 82,000. The supplier is unapproved. The service will
process restricted information using external SaaS. The contract is for
12 months. No exception has been approved."


L2 — NATURALISTIC

The same facts are expressed as an ordinary business request or description.

Facts may be combined into sentences and expressed using natural business
language, but every policy-relevant fact must remain clear and unambiguous.

Example style:

"The operations team wants a one-year subscription to an externally hosted
SaaS service costing USD 82,000. The provider has not completed supplier
approval and the service will handle information classified as restricted.
No exception has been approved."


L3 — DISPERSED

The same facts are distributed through a longer business narrative.

Policy-relevant facts may appear in a less convenient order and may be
separated by limited policy-irrelevant contextual information.

Every policy-relevant fact must nevertheless remain recoverable and
unambiguous.

Example style:

"The operations team is considering a new service for its regional workflow.
The proposed provider has not completed supplier approval. The subscription
would run for one year. Its total value is USD 82,000. The team expects
information classified as restricted to be processed in the provider's
externally hosted SaaS environment. No exception has been approved."


GENERAL CONSTRAINTS

1. Every rendering must preserve the canonical case exactly.

2. A rendering must not add, remove, or alter a policy-relevant fact.

3. A rendering must not introduce genuine ambiguity.

4. A rendering must not require external knowledge to determine a
   policy-relevant fact.

5. Approximate monetary values must not replace exact canonical values.

6. Exception status and scope must remain explicit enough to map
   unambiguously to the canonical case.

7. Deployment must remain unambiguous. Terms such as "cloud-hosted" alone
   must not be treated as equivalent to external SaaS.

8. Data classification must remain unambiguous. A rendering must not require
   the model to infer classification from the type of information described.

9. Supplier status must remain unambiguous.

10. Contract duration must remain unambiguous.

11. L3 may contain limited irrelevant context, but that context must not
    introduce conflicting or potentially policy-relevant facts.

12. Renderings must not contain worked policy reasoning, control names,
    expected decisions, hints about applicable rules, or ground-truth answers.

13. Linguistic complexity and linguistic ambiguity are distinct. E1 varies
    presentation complexity while deliberately avoiding genuine linguistic
    ambiguity.

14. The three renderings of a case form a cluster and must retain identifiers:
    Cxx-L1, Cxx-L2, and Cxx-L3.

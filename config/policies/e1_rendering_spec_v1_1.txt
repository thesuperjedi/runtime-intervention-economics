E1 Rendering Specification v1.1
Status: CANDIDATE — not frozen

Purpose
Create controlled natural-language renderings of each canonical E1 case while preserving the authoritative policy-relevant facts exactly.

General requirements
1. Every rendering must remain semantically equivalent to its corresponding canonical case.
2. Do not add, remove, alter, infer, or contradict any policy-relevant fact.
3. Do not introduce genuine ambiguity or require external knowledge.
4. Exact threshold-sensitive quantities must remain exact.
5. Supplier status, data classification, deployment, contract duration, exception approval status, and exception scope must remain unambiguous.
6. Do not state, imply, or explain the policy controls or answer.
7. Rendering IDs use Cxx-L1, Cxx-L2, and Cxx-L3.

L1 — EXPLICIT
Present the canonical facts directly and compactly. L1 may be highly standardized and field-like in prose form. Its purpose is primarily to test policy application with minimal linguistic extraction burden.

L2 — NATURALISTIC
Express the same canonical facts in ordinary, compact business-request language. Facts may be combined into natural sentences, but must remain clear and unambiguous. Do not add worked reasoning, policy hints, or conclusions.

L3 — DISPERSED NATURALISTIC
Purpose:
Test extraction and integration of the same authoritative policy-relevant facts when those facts are distributed through a realistic business narrative rather than presented in a compact or field-like form.

Requirements:
1. Every policy-relevant fact must remain semantically equivalent to the corresponding canonical case.
2. All policy-relevant facts must remain definite and unambiguous.
3. Policy-relevant facts should be distributed across the narrative rather than presented as a sequence of one-fact-per-sentence statements.
4. Facts may appear in a less convenient order than L1 or L2.
5. Limited policy-irrelevant business context may occur between relevant facts when it makes the request more natural.
6. Some sentences may contain more than one policy-relevant fact, while related facts may occur in different parts of the narrative.
7. L3 should normally require information to be integrated from multiple parts of the text.
8. Additional context must not introduce new policy-relevant facts, contradict canonical facts, imply missing facts, or require external knowledge.
9. L3 must not become genuinely ambiguous. Increased extraction effort is not permission to obscure whether a canonical fact is true.
10. L3 must not state, imply, or explain which policy controls follow from the facts.
11. L3 must not be created merely by taking L1 and:
    - adding an introductory sentence;
    - expanding individual noun phrases;
    - rearranging otherwise isolated fact sentences;
    - increasing word count without changing information distribution.
12. L3 need not be syntactically complex. Difficulty should arise primarily from information distribution and integration, not convoluted grammar.
13. Exact threshold-sensitive quantities must remain exact. For example, USD 50,000 must not become "about USD 50,000", and 24 months must not become "roughly two years".
14. Categorical policy facts must remain explicit enough to map unambiguously to the canonical state, particularly supplier status, data classification, internal versus external SaaS deployment, exception approval status, and exception scope.
15. Exception scope may be expressed using different natural-language formulations across levels, but the rendering must describe the scope of the exception rather than state the resulting policy consequence.
16. Linguistic variation should be controlled. L3 renderings should not all share a single fixed sentence skeleton, but diversity must not be added merely for stylistic novelty.

Design principle
Facts(L1) = Facts(L2) = Facts(L3)

Presentation conditions:
L1 = explicit fact presentation
L2 = compact natural business request
L3 = dispersed natural business narrative

L3 is not defined as "harder" by assumption. Whether models actually find L3 harder is an empirical result.
